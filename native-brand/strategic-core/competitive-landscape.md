## The four camps a buyer compares Native to

When a founder or CEO is deciding how to move, they're weighing Native against one of four familiar shapes. Each has a promise, a limit, and a story we have to counter.

### 1. Management consultancies (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, Accenture)

- **Promise:** Strategy-grade thinking, senior partners, frameworks, Fortune-500 references.
- **Deliverable:** A deck, a roadmap, a slide track. Execution sold separately.
- **Where they win:** Board-level legitimacy. Regulated industries. Questions where "thinking" is the product.
- **Where they lose:** Installation. The recommendations sit in a drawer because no one on the ground owns the rebuild. Cost structure is incompatible with sub-$50M companies.
- **Messaging gap:** They talk about transformation. They don't talk about *living inside* the transformation.

### 2. Digital agencies and branding studios (Huge, R/GA, Pentagram, Work & Co, Red Antler)

- **Promise:** Beautiful brand, website, campaign, product surface.
- **Deliverable:** Artifacts. Polished, defensible, handed off.
- **Where they win:** Aesthetic and craft. Launches. Moments in time.
- **Where they lose:** Strategy upstream of the artifact, and systems downstream of it. The brand looks right for a quarter, then drifts because no one maintains the strategic core it was generated from.
- **Messaging gap:** They sell artifacts. We sell the system the artifacts come out of.

### 3. Venture studios and startup studios (AtomicPD, Expa, Human Ventures, Science Inc.)

- **Promise:** We build companies with founders. Shared cap structure. Operator DNA.
- **Deliverable:** Varies widely. Some are incubators, some are capital-first, few are full-stack operator teams.
- **Where they win:** Speed of formation. Access to capital. Founder community.
- **Where they lose:** Depth of operating muscle once the company is live. Thin on brand, GTM, and systems. Almost none are AI-Native by default.
- **Messaging gap:** They sell company formation. We sell the system the company runs on after formation.

### 4. Dev shops and AI implementers (Thoughtbot, Reify, a16z's enterprise crew, freelancers on Upwork)

- **Promise:** We'll build the product, the integration, the automation, the agent.
- **Deliverable:** Code. Sometimes prompts. Sometimes agents.
- **Where they win:** Execution on a spec.
- **Where they lose:** Without a strategic core upstream, the code solves the wrong problem beautifully. AI implementation without positioning, ICP, and narrative is expensive theater.
- **Messaging gap:** They sell the tools. We sell the playbook the tools plug into.

## The real competitor: "We'll figure it out internally."

Most deals are lost not to another firm but to the internal option — hire a Head of Growth, hire a CMO, lean on the existing team, buy a few tools, figure it out.

- **Where internal wins:** When the company has a full bench of operators with AI-Native muscle memory, and the time to install the system themselves.
- **Where internal loses:** When the CEO is bandwidth-constrained, the team is delivering rather than building, and the market window is closing faster than the hiring plan. Which is almost always.

Our job is to show, in the first call, that the internal path is slower and more expensive than it looks once you price the strategic cost of delay.

## Where the messaging white space is

None of the four camps sits in the intersection of:

1. **Strategy-grade thinking** (like consultancies)
2. **Craft and brand** (like agencies)
3. **Company-building DNA** (like venture studios)
4. **AI-Native operating model** (like the better dev shops)

...all delivered by a single embedded team that owns the outcome.

That intersection is the category we occupy. We don't call it a new category — we call it what it is: **the operator team that builds AI-Native companies.**
