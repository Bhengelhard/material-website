# icp.md

> Your ideal customer. Who you sell to, how to recognize them, and what makes a tier-one fit.

---

## Native serves two customer shapes, one for each mode.

### ICP A — Build with us

**The industry-native operator launching a new company.**

- Has spent 7–20 years inside an industry. Knows the buyer, the incumbents, and where they're soft.
- Has conviction on a specific wedge — a product, a channel, a market shift — and credibility to execute on it.
- Wants to build, not advise. Has decided they're starting the company, the only questions are *how fast* and *with whom*.
- Capital-light at the start. Needs a team and a playbook more than they need a check.
- Willing to share equity/cap table in exchange for speed, structure, and an operator co-pilot.

**Tier-one signal — pick the top three:**
- [ ] Operator-founder (not first-time dabbler). Comes from inside the industry they're attacking.
- [ ] Has a clear wedge. Can describe the first customer, the first channel, and the first year in plain English.
- [ ] Unblocked on distribution or domain. The relationships or the knowledge already exist.
- [ ] Moving in weeks, not quarters. "We want to be live this year" energy.
- [ ] Open to a venture-studio cap structure. Comfortable with shared risk and shared upside.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Pure-play tech founder looking for an outsourced engineering team.
- Idea-stage without conviction. Needs us to tell them what to build.
- Expects fully funded operations without putting skin in the game.

---

### ICP B — Modernize & scale

**The CEO or founder of a 7–9 figure business scaling through the messy middle.**

- Revenue $2M–$50M. Team 15–150. Past product-market fit, not yet at repeatable scale.
- Feels the gap between where the company is and where AI-Native companies are headed. Doesn't have the internal bandwidth or the muscle memory to close it.
- Has tried consultants (left with a deck) or agencies (left with deliverables), but the business didn't change.
- Looking for an embedded team, not a report. Wants operators inside the business, with skin in the outcome.

**Tier-one signal — pick the top three:**
- [ ] CEO or founder still in the seat. Decision-maker on strategy *and* systems.
- [ ] Growing, but the playbook is breaking. GTM motion, team structure, or ops can't scale another 2x without a rebuild.
- [ ] Budget authority for a multi-quarter embedded engagement. Not looking for a one-off sprint.
- [ ] Open to rewriting the strategic core, not just the collateral. Willing to question positioning, ICP, and narrative.
- [ ] Has the raw material for AI to compound on — data, pipeline, product surface area.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Seed-stage startup looking for a brand refresh.
- Enterprise looking for a procurement-shaped deliverable.
- Leader who wants AI cosmetics without strategy change underneath.

## How we recognize a fit in the first call

Three questions, answered honestly:

1. **"What's broken that you can't fix with another hire?"** — Tells us whether the problem is strategic or operational. Native only works when it's strategic.
2. **"If we ran this right for the next 6–12 months, what would be true that isn't true today?"** — Tells us whether the CEO or founder has a picture of the end state, or just pain in the current state.
3. **"Who on the leadership team is going to push back on this?"** — Tells us whether the organization is ready for an embedded operator team, or will treat us as outsiders.

If all three answers are sharp and specific, it's a tier-one fit. If any one is vague, we clarify before we engage.

---

## Not our ICP (on purpose)

- Pre-conviction founders who need validation, not execution.
- Enterprises shopping a procurement-shaped SOW.
- Companies that want the brand without the system underneath.
- Leaders looking for an AI-flavored marketing refresh and nothing more.

We'd rather say no than build on a bad foundation.
