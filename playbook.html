How to build an AI-Native company in 2026.

A practical walkthrough for founders and operators. Humans lead the strategy. AI runs the execution. Experts shape the review. The order you build things in matters more than the tools you pick.

How building happens now

Ideas. Execution. Review.

Every piece of work a company produces, a pitch, a landing page, an email sequence, a product feature, a dashboard, moves through these three stages. The shift of the last two years: the middle stage now runs on AI, while strategy and judgment stay with people. That changes what a small team can do.

Stage 01 · Human

Ideas and strategy

Positioning, narrative, priorities, product vision, what to build next. The things AI still can't do well. This is where experienced operators earn their keep.

Stage 02 · AI

Execution

Drafts the email, writes the brief, generates the landing page, cleans the data, enriches the lead, updates the CRM, ships the first cut of code. The 80% that used to consume a team.

Stage 03 · Human + experts

Review and judgment

Approves the sequence, tunes the copy, picks the winning variant, shapes what actually ships. Experts across brand, product, sales, and marketing guide the work, rather than do the work.

This is how Material works. Our team leads strategy and review. AI handles execution in between. You get the quality of a seasoned operator with the velocity of a ten-person team.

Where AI fits in

Three pieces do the work.

To put AI into the execution stage, you give it three things. Treat it like a new hire on their first day: they need to know what the company is, how you do specific things, and when to do them. The whole playbook is built on these three pieces.

01

Context, the files the AI reads.

Your positioning, tone of voice, ICP, processes, and playbooks live as plain text files in a shared folder (Google Drive works well). Point Claude Cowork or Claude Code at that folder, and every team member and every agent builds off the same decisions. Change a file once, every downstream output updates. No re-explaining the company with every new prompt.

02

Skills, reusable ways of doing.

A skill is a trained recipe for one thing. "Write an outbound email in our voice." "Qualify a lead against our ICP." "Draft a blog post from a brief." You define it once and the AI decides when to load it on its own, based on what you're asking. You don't have to specify which skill to use. Skills turn one-off prompts into repeatable, consistent work.

03

Agents, routines that run on their own.

An agent is a scheduled or triggered routine that combines context and skills into a process. An agent can kickstart your tasks each morning, keep your CRM clean throughout the day, or qualify every inbound lead the moment it arrives. You set the job once and it runs without you, every day, at the pace of the business.

Tooling

Use Claude Cowork and/or Claude Code for execution. They give you context, skills, and agents in one workspace, and sit on top of the tools your team already uses: Google Drive, Notion, HubSpot, Gmail, Slack, whatever. The agents reach into those tools on your behalf. Nothing to rip and replace, no engineering team required to get started.

The five phases

Built in order, one layer at a time.

Skip a phase and the later ones break. Follow them and the system compounds. Start small, expand based on what's working.

The order matters
more than any single step. Each phase earns the next. If you only take one thing from this playbook, take the order.
Phase 1

Strategic core

Start by writing the markdown files that describe what your company is. These files become the foundation every phase downstream reads from. Keep them in one shared folder so every person and every agent works from the same source.

  • core-concept.mdThe soul of the offering in one page. What it is, what it does, and why it exists.
  • icp.mdYour ideal customer. Who you sell to, how to recognize them, and what makes a tier-one fit.
  • competitive-landscape.mdHow alternatives position themselves. Where the messaging gaps are. Where you can play.
  • positioning.mdHow you frame yourself inside that landscape. Your place in the market and your claim on it.
  • narrative-design.mdThe story arc behind every pitch, page, and post. Setup, conflict, resolution, proof.
Phase 2

Brand, materials, and marketing website

Turn the strategic core into the things a customer or investor actually sees. Identity, voice, collateral, a simple landing page. Each piece is generated from the same markdown, so they stay consistent with each other and with what you'll build next.

  • brand-style-guide.mdThe complete brand system in one file. Colors, type, marks, logo usage rules, and visual do and don't examples. Every agent and designer reads from it.
  • tone-of-voice.mdHow the brand sounds. Voice principles, word bank, do and don't examples. Becomes the voice every agent writes in.
  • Pitch deckNarrative-driven deck for sales, investors, and partnerships. Derived directly from the positioning and narrative files.
  • One-pagerSingle-page leave-behind. Email attachments, event follow-ups, post-meeting recaps.
  • Marketing websiteStart with a simple landing page. Iterate from there as the story sharpens and the audience clarifies.
Pro tip
After an advisor call, paste the transcript or your notes into your AI workspace as context. It can read your core files, weigh the advisor's feedback against them, and update the right markdown files (and the website copy) to reflect what changed. No more "good advice" that never actually ships.
Phase 3

Minimum loveable product or service

Get the systems, tech, and team in place to actually deliver the thing you sell. Not a barely-working MVP, but the minimum version customers will love. AI is baked in where it makes the experience faster, sharper, or more personal, not as a feature tacked on the side.

  • Product or service itselfThe minimum loveable version of what you sell. Scoped small enough to ship, good enough that a real customer would happily pay for it.
  • AI enablement layerThe AI working inside the product or service. What it does for the customer, where it speeds up delivery, how it personalizes the experience. Part of the value, not a feature.
  • Design partnersTwo to five early customers who agree to use the product, give feedback often, and help shape what gets built. They get early access and a direct line to the team. You get real-world validation before launch.
  • Team to deliverThe humans needed to ship and run it. Strategy, delivery, support, whatever the shape of the business demands.
  • CRM and onboarding pipelinesSystem of record plus the flows that take a new customer from sign-up to successfully using the product.
  • Internal toolingDashboards, admin interfaces, and analytics so the team can see what's happening and act on it. GA4, PostHog, Meta Pixel, Search Console wired up from day one.
Phase 4

Activate traction channels

Phases 1 through 3 used AI to help you build the foundation. Phase 4 is where AI starts doing repeatable work for the business. Each channel is a self-contained engine: a short strategy, a few skills, a few agents, and the human checkpoints that keep quality high. Pick two or three to start. Expand based on what the reporting layer tells you is working.

  • OutboundCold email and cold call. Reach prospects you've identified directly.
  • Content engineBlog, newsletter, social. One good draft recycled into many formats.
  • SEOOn-page, programmatic, and technical. Discoverable over time, tracked week over week.
  • Social engagementCommenting, DMs, and connection building on the platforms your buyers live on.
  • Paid acquisitionGoogle, Meta, LinkedIn ads pointed at landing pages built from your positioning.
  • Community and listeningReddit, HN, industry Slacks. Show up with value, catch buying signals, show up again.
  • PartnershipsStrategic, integration, and co-marketing. Different cadence and tone than sales.
  • Go-to-networkWarm intros through team, investor, and advisor graphs mapped against your ICP.
  • Referral programHappy customers identified. Asks surfaced to sales at the right moments.
  • Events and speakingScouting, prep, follow-up, and turning every recording into more content.
  • Sales enablementTranscript mining, deal acceleration, content surfacing, and close notifications.
  • PR and reviewsJournalist outreach and review-site generation. Highest-stakes comms.

Example skills

Reusable recipes the AI produces on demand. You ask, it writes.

  • Outbound
    Outbound copywriter. Writes a cold email in your voice using core, positioning, and tone files.
  • Content
    Content brief generator. Turns a topic into a full brief with keyword map, outline, and angle.
  • Paid
    Landing page generator. Conversion-optimized page built from positioning and a specific ad or campaign.

Example agents

Routines that run on a schedule or a trigger. You don't ask, they just do it. Output is a ranked shortlist, not a firehose of alerts.

  • Outbound
    Buying-signal hunter. Scans target accounts for funding rounds, job changes, tech stack shifts, and competitor churn. Every morning, surfaces the top five accounts worth outreach today with the angle for each.
  • Content
    Content drafter. Watches the editorial calendar and drafts every upcoming post a few days before it's due. Your team starts from a near-final draft, not a blank page.
  • SEO
    Content gap finder. Each week, finds topics your competitors rank for but you don't cover. Drafts briefs for the handful worth pursuing.
Phase 5

Reporting and feedback loop

Every week, the system does three things. It reports on what's working. It recommends what to change. It writes the approved changes back into the core files and the channel skills. This is how a company that launched last month keeps getting sharper every month after.

01 · Reports

A weekly pulse to the team

A one-page summary every Monday morning. Metrics by channel, pipeline status, live experiments, agent output. The team reads it in two minutes and knows what's moving.

02 · Recommendations

Proposed revisions for review

ICP refinements, winning copy patterns, channel shifts, new objections worth addressing. The team sees each recommendation with the data behind it and approves, edits, or dismisses.

03 · Feedback loop

Approved changes update the system

Updates write into the Phase 1 markdown files and the Phase 4 skills. Where a pattern recurs, a new agent spins up for it. Next week's execution runs on the sharper version.

Example skills

Reusable recipes the AI produces on demand. One per stage.

  • Report
    Pulse report writer. Turns raw metrics from CRM and analytics tools into a two-minute readable summary for the team.
  • Recommend
    Recommendation writer. Structures a proposed change with the evidence behind it. Easy to approve, edit, or dismiss.
  • Feedback
    Transcript analyzer. Reads a sales call and extracts objections, pain language, and win signals worth capturing.

Example agents

Routines that run on a schedule, without being asked. One per stage.

  • Report
    Weekly pulse. Every Monday, pulls channel metrics, pipeline, and live experiments. Writes a one-page summary that explains what changed and why, not just the numbers.
  • Recommend
    Channel rebalancer. Tracks CAC, pipeline velocity, and win rate across channels. When one shifts meaningfully for two weeks in a row, proposes a reallocation with the evidence to back it up.
  • Feedback
    ICP learner. Each month, studies closed-won and closed-lost deals. Proposes updates to your ICP file based on which segments actually converted.
The full picture

Put it all together.

This is what building a company AI-Native actually looks like when you stop thinking about tools and start thinking about order.

  1. Write the strategic core.A handful of markdown files that explain what the company is, who it's for, and how it wins. Everything downstream reads from them.
  2. Dress it up.Brand, voice, pitch deck, one-pager, and a simple marketing website. All generated from the same core, so they stay in sync.
  3. Build the minimum loveable product or service.The thing you actually sell, with AI baked in where it makes the experience better, a team to deliver it, and the systems that run the operation.
  4. Turn on two or three channels.Reusable skills and agents do the execution. Humans and experts guide strategy and review. Scale what works.
  5. Close the loop.Weekly reports show what's working. Feedback agents update the core files and the channel skills. The whole system gets smarter on its own.

Do this, in this order, and you get the quality of a seasoned operator with the velocity of a ten-person team, on a system that gets sharper every week instead of staler.

The edge isn't the AI. It's the strategic core the AI is built on.
